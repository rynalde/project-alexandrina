### The input: a clip

Same recipe as I-JEPA, adapted for time. V-JEPA takes **16 frames**, sampled every 4th frame (about 3 seconds of video), at 224×224.

### Tokens: from patches to tubelets

Images were cut into 2D patches. Video is cut into <TermTip term="tubelets">3D patches spanning space and time</TermTip>, each covering **2 frames × 16 × 16 pixels**. So 16 frames become 8 time steps, each a 14×14 grid:

**8 × 14 × 14 = 1,568 tokens** per clip.

<TokenCounter />

### Masking: hide ~90%

<PredictReveal prompt="MAE hides 75% of an image. Why would V-JEPA hide as much as 90% of a video?">
Video is extremely redundant — neighbouring frames and neighbouring patches are nearly identical. With light masking, the model can fill gaps by copying and interpolating, which needs no understanding. Heavy masking forces it to model what's actually going on.
</PredictReveal>

Each clip gets **two masks**, each its own prediction task: a **short-range** mask (the union of 8 blocks, each ~15% of a frame) and a **long-range** mask (the union of 2 blocks, each ~70%). Blocks overlap and merge, so the average masking ratio comes out at about **90%**.

### The key trick: the same mask in every frame

The spatial blocks are **repeated along the whole time axis** — a "tube" through the clip. If each frame had its own mask, a hidden patch would often be visible at the same spot one frame earlier. The model would copy it and learn nothing about motion.

### The whole pipeline

A clip cut into tubelets, a tube mask, two encoders, a predictor and one L1 loss. Step through it, then turn on the gradient flow to see which half actually trains.

<VJepaFigure />

<details>
<summary>Go deeper: everything else in the V-JEPA recipe</summary>

- Loss: **L1** between predicted and target features (I-JEPA used L2).
- Target encoder: EMA, momentum **0.998 → 1.0**.
- Predictor: a narrow transformer, 12 blocks, width 384.
- Data: **VideoMix2M** — about 2M videos from HowTo100M, Kinetics-400/600/700 and Something-Something-v2.
- Results (frozen backbone, attentive probe, largest model ViT-H/16 at 384px): **81.9%** Kinetics-400, **72.2%** Something-Something-v2, **77.4%** ImageNet with the standard probe (77.9% with a two-layer probe).

</details>

### V-JEPA 2 (2025): the same idea, scaled

- **Data**: over 1M hours of video plus 1M images (VideoMix22M).
- **Model**: up to ViT-g, over 1B parameters.
- **Positions**: <Highlight>3D-RoPE</Highlight> — rotary position embeddings applied separately to time, height and width.
- **Recipe**: train mostly on short, low-resolution clips, then cool down briefly on longer, sharper ones — about 8× cheaper.

<details>
<summary>Go deeper: V-JEPA 2's numbers, and V-JEPA 2.1</summary>

- **Progressive resolution**: mostly 16 frames at 256px, then a short cooldown at 64 frames (384px for the ViT-g/384 model). The EMA momentum and weight decay stay fixed instead of following a schedule.
- **Results**: 77.3% on Something-Something-v2, 39.7 recall@5 on Epic-Kitchens-100 action anticipation; aligned with a language model, 84.0 on PerceptionTest.

**V-JEPA 2.1 (2026)** keeps the recipe but asks for *dense* features: the loss uses both visible and masked tokens. It reports 77.7 on Something-Something-v2, 0.307 RMSE for depth on NYUv2 with a linear probe, and 20 points better real-robot grasping than V-JEPA 2-AC.

</details>
