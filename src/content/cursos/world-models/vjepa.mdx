### The input: a clip

V-JEPA takes **16 frames**, sampled every 4th frame (about 3 seconds of video), at 224×224.

### Tokens: from patches to tubelets

Images were cut into 2D patches. Video is cut into <TermTip term="tubelets">3D patches spanning space and time</TermTip>: each one covers **2 frames × 16 × 16 pixels**. So 16 frames become 8 time steps, and each frame a 14×14 grid:

**8 × 14 × 14 = 1,568 tokens** per clip.

<TokenCounter />

### Masking: hide ~90%

Each clip gets **two masks**, and each one is its own prediction task: a **short-range** mask (the union of 8 blocks, each ~15% of a frame) and a **long-range** mask (the union of 2 blocks, each ~70%). Because blocks overlap and are merged, the average masking ratio comes out at about **90%**.

<PredictReveal prompt="MAE hides 75% of an image. Why does V-JEPA hide as much as 90% of a video?">
Video is extremely redundant — neighbouring frames and neighbouring patches are nearly identical. With light masking, the model can fill gaps by copying and interpolating, which needs no understanding. Heavy masking forces it to model what's actually going on.
</PredictReveal>

### The key trick: the same mask in every frame

The spatial blocks are **repeated across the whole time axis** — a "tube" through the clip. If each frame had its own mask, a hidden patch would often be visible at the same spot one frame earlier. The model would copy it and learn nothing about motion. The interaction on the next screen lets you count those leaks.

### Everything else

- — Loss: **L1** between predicted and target features (I-JEPA used L2).
- — Target encoder: EMA, momentum **0.998 → 1.0**.
- — Predictor: narrow transformer, 12 blocks, width 384.
- — Data: **VideoMix2M** — about 2M videos from HowTo100M, Kinetics-400/600/700 and Something-Something-v2.
- — Results (frozen backbone, attentive probe, largest model ViT-H/16 at 384px): **81.9%** Kinetics-400, **72.2%** Something-Something-v2, **77.4%** ImageNet with the standard probe (77.9% with a two-layer probe).

### V-JEPA 2 (2025): the same idea, scaled

- — **Data**: over 1M hours of video plus 1M images (VideoMix22M).
- — **Model**: up to ViT-g, over 1B parameters.
- — **Positions**: <Highlight>3D-RoPE</Highlight> — rotary position embeddings applied separately to time, height and width.
- — **Recipe**: progressive resolution — train mostly at 16 frames / 256px, then a short cooldown with longer, higher-resolution clips (64 frames; 384px for the ViT-g/384 model). About 8× cheaper. The EMA momentum and weight decay stay fixed instead of following a schedule.
- — **Results**: 77.3% on Something-Something-v2, 39.7 recall@5 on Epic-Kitchens-100 action anticipation; aligned with a language model it reaches 84.0 on PerceptionTest.

**V-JEPA 2.1 (2026)** keeps the family recipe but asks for *dense* features: the loss now uses both visible and masked tokens. It reports 77.7 on Something-Something-v2, 0.307 RMSE for depth on NYUv2 with a linear probe, and 20 points better real-robot grasping than V-JEPA 2-AC.

</Body>

</Callout>
