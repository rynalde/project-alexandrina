### Branch A — predict pixels

**World Models (Ha &amp; Schmidhuber, 2018).** V, a VAE, compresses each frame; M, a recurrent network, predicts the next compressed frame; C, a tiny controller, acts. In VizDoom the controller trained entirely inside the model's "dream".

**Dreamer (Hafner et al., 2020 → DreamerV3, 2023).** Learns latent dynamics with a pixel-reconstruction loss, then learns to act by imagining futures. DreamerV3 collected diamonds in Minecraft from scratch.

**MAE (He et al., 2021).** Not a video world model, but the masking blueprint: hide 75% of an image's patches and reconstruct their pixels.

### Branch B — predict representations

**BYOL (Grill et al., 2020).** Predict one view's representation from another's: EMA target, predictor, no negatives — chapter 03's recipe.

**data2vec (Baevski et al., 2022).** A student sees a masked input and predicts the representations an EMA teacher makes of the full input, for speech, images and text. The I-JEPA paper names it as its closest prior work, with Context Autoencoders.

**JEPA (LeCun, 2022).** A position paper, *A Path Towards Autonomous Machine Intelligence*: predict in representation space, stack JEPAs into a hierarchy, and put that world model at the centre of an agent.

**I-JEPA (2023) → V-JEPA (2024) → V-JEPA 2 (2025).** Meta's implementations: images, then video, then video at scale with robot planning. Chapters 05–07 open each one up.

### The 2024–2026 wave

Branch B kept growing fast: planners built on frozen features, a "surprise" test of intuitive physics, SIGReg's LeJEPA, and JEPA world models trained end to end from pixels. Branch A answered with NVIDIA's **Cosmos** (Jan 2025), open-weight models that generate future video — and DreamerV3 appeared in *Nature* in 2025.

<details>
<summary>Go deeper: seven papers, one line each</summary>

Each line was checked against the paper's abstract.

- **DINO-WM (Nov 2024).** A world model on frozen DINOv2 features, trained on offline trajectories. It plans toward a goal image by searching in feature space — no reward model, no expert demonstrations, no pixel reconstruction.
- **Intuitive physics (Feb 2025).** Show a possible and an impossible clip; "surprise" = prediction error. V-JEPA shows object permanence and shape consistency where pixel predictors and multimodal LLMs stay closer to chance. A reality check followed: on IntPhys 2 (Jun 2025), in complex scenes most models are still near chance while humans are near-perfect.
- **LeJEPA (Nov 2025).** SIGReg replaces EMA and stop-gradient: one hyperparameter, about 50 lines of code, 79% on ImageNet with a ViT-H/14.
- **VL-JEPA (Dec 2025).** Predicts the *embedding* of an answer instead of generating its words: 50% fewer trainable parameters than a comparable token-predicting model.
- **V-JEPA 2.1 (Mar 2026).** Dense features — the loss uses visible and masked tokens — and 20 points better real-robot grasping than V-JEPA 2-AC.
- **LeWorldModel (Mar 2026).** An end-to-end JEPA world model from raw pixels with just two losses (next-embedding prediction + SIGReg): about 15M parameters, one GPU, a few hours.
- **LeVJEPA (Aug 2026).** SIGReg for video pretraining: at ViT-S/B/L scale, on the same data and epochs, it matches or beats V-JEPA 2 with 5.6–20.8× less pretraining compute.

</details>

### What JEPA inherited

- From <Highlight>World Models and Dreamer</Highlight>: the goal — a model of the world an agent can plan with.
- From <Highlight>MAE</Highlight>: masking as the pretext task, at scale, with ViTs.
- From <Highlight>BYOL</Highlight>: the EMA target + predictor that prevents collapse.
- From <Highlight>LeCun 2022</Highlight>: the insistence on predicting in representation space.
